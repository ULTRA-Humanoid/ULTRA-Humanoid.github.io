ULTRA: Unified Multimodal Control for Autonomous Humanoid Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation

Xialin He*, Sirui Xu*, Xinyao Li, Runpei Dong, Liuyu Bian,
Yu-Xiong Wang†, Liang-Yan Gui†
University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign
*Equal Contribution   †Equal Advising
Paper arXiv

ULTRA is an all-in-one controller for humanoid loco-manipulation that supports dense motion tracking, fine-grained control, and long-horizon goal following with egocentric perception.

Abstract

Achieving autonomous and versatile whole-body loco-manipulation remains a central barrier to making humanoids practically useful. Yet existing approaches remain fundamentally constrained: retargeted data is often scarce or low-quality, methods struggle to scale to large skill repertoires, and, most importantly, they operate by tracking predefined motion references rather than generating behavior from perception and high-level task specifications. To this end, we propose ULTRA, a unified framework with two key components. First, we introduce a physics-driven retargeting algorithm that translates large-scale motion capture into humanoid embodiments while preserving physical plausibility for contact-rich interactions. Second, we learn a unified multimodal controller that supports both dense reference and sparse task specifications, under sensing ranging from accurate motion-capture state to noisy egocentric visual inputs. To achieve this, we distill a universal tracking policy into this controller, compress motor skills into a compact latent space, and apply RL finetuning to expand skill coverage and improve robustness under out-of-distribution scenarios. This enables coordinated whole-body behavior from sparse intent without reference motion at test time. We evaluate ULTRA in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid robot. Results show that ULTRA generalizes to autonomous, goal-conditioned whole-body loco-manipulation from egocentric perception, consistently outperforming tracking-only baselines with limited skills.
